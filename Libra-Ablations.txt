Libra: fallback, uncorrelated matching, and Hamming-weight experiments
Follow-up to Libra.txt and Libra-PyMatching.txt; existing files are preserved.
Experiment date: 2026-10-07. Only new project files were created.

Decisions
=========
Retain the extra high-gap unconstrained correlated MWPM call in original Libra.
Removing it increased the d9 observed failures from 20 to 23 on identical shots.
Keep uncorrelated matching as an experimental configuration: it was faster and
beat ordinary PyMatching at d5/d7/d9, but had more failures than original Libra.
An adaptive detector-HW rule is promising at d5. Conservative d7/d9 thresholds
cover about 12% of shots; their low-HW samples show no failures for any decoder,
so they do not establish a low-HW advantage over PyMatching.

Model, pairing, and meanings
============================
Surface-code SI1000, physical error rate p=0.001, Z memory, rounds=distance,
opposing basis detectors enabled. Monte Carlo samples the decomposed Stim DEM.
Every comparison uses exactly the same sampled syndrome and logical truth for
both Libra variants and ordinary uncorrelated PyMatching. Truth only scores errors.
Accuracy runs use 64 worker threads on CPUs 0-63, stopping after both Libra
variants have at least 20 failures, or at 10 million requested shots. A weaker
variant naturally accumulates more than 25 failures while original Libra reaches
20. These are observed counts and point estimates; no significance claim is made.
Worker sampling streams use seed + 0x9e3779b97f4a7c15*(worker+1). Scheduling can
change stream lengths and the endpoint. Each failed shot records worker/local
index, detector and error-channel IDs for replay. Decoders always share each shot.
Ensemble: 60 members, gap threshold 20 dB, 30 positive cycles/class, degeneracy on.
Selection data: sample seed=ensemble seed=20261001. Fixed-prior holdout: sample
seed=20261002, ensemble seed=20261001. A supplemental run also changes ensemble
seed to 20261002; it is reported separately.
Detector Hamming weight (HW) is the total number of fired detectors in BOTH
bases over the ENTIRE d-round history. It is observed and usable for dispatch.
DEM fault HW counts sampled independent DEM channels. It is a simulation
diagnostic, unavailable to a real decoder, and not an exact physical gate-fault
count. Channels can combine physical mechanisms or multiple graph components.

d5 tied in total failures, exchanging one error each. d7 without the call had
20 versus 22 failures. d9 without it had 23 versus 20, with five newly incorrect
shots and two newly corrected shots. The condition to remove it at all three
distances was not met. Existing stable original Libra remains unchanged.
The no-fallback configuration is still available for experiments.
On the large-gap branch it returns the lower original-hyperedge-weight
constrained representative; the small-gap branch is unchanged. Each candidate
call contains correlated two-pass matching, edge recovery, and exact auxiliary
hyperedge assignment. The two complementary candidate calls remain the main
cost: previous phase profiles assign 79%-94% of total Libra time to them. Those
timers do not separate matching from edge recovery and assignment.

2. Replacing correlated matching with uncorrelated matching
===========================================================
Only matching changes: graphs disable correlation rules and all complementary,
ensemble, and large-gap fallback candidates use PyMatching single-pass MWPM.
Hyperedge assignment, correlated channel weights, perturbations, synthesis,
degeneracy, and fallback policy are retained. This variant still uses correlation
information when assigning/scoring hyperedges. It is not a fully independent
fault model. Changing matching can change the gap and ensemble trigger.

4. Adaptive rule: freeze thresholds, then test independent samples
==================================================================
Rule: use uncorrelated Libra when detector HW <= T_d, otherwise original Libra.
Selection rule: largest threshold with zero uncorrelated-only failures below it
in the first dataset. Frozen thresholds: T_5=8, T_7=5, T_9=15.
Thresholds were saved before holdout sampling. They were not retuned afterwards.

At d5, low-HW uncorrelated Libra matched original Libra (12 errors each) and
beat PyMatching (18). It corrected one original-only failure at HW=4 and added
one failure at HW=6. Thus the equal-error-count property held on these samples;
the stronger zero-additional-failures property from selection did not generalize.
At d7/d9, all low-HW decoders had zero holdout errors. These bins support a
conservative option with limited coverage, but no strict superiority over PM.
All three adaptive complete-history error totals matched original Libra and
were lower than complete-history PyMatching totals (32, 61, and 48).
These are candidate operating thresholds at p=0.001 and rounds=d, not guaranteed
bounds or validated thresholds for other rates, rounds, bases, or distances.

5. Decoding latency and adaptive timing estimate
================================================
8192 paired Monte Carlo shots per distance; CPU 62; sample seed=20261003,
ensemble seed=20261001; graph and ensemble preparation outside timing; validation
and phase profiling off. Alternating which Libra variant decodes first. Times
cover the ENTIRE d-round history, NOT one round. No simulation/transpose/HW-count
time is included. These runs measure timing only, not rare failure rates.
d  corr us  unc us   unc speedup  adaptive us  adaptive saving  PM us 
-  -------  -------  -----------  -----------  ---------------  ------
5  67.599   35.135   1.92x        40.568       39.99%           1.971 
7  191.045  99.103   1.93x        180.943      5.29%            6.583 
9  420.599  212.495  1.98x        397.422      5.51%            14.436
Adaptive time is the offline sum of the selected per-shot measured decoder
times. Predictions were likewise selected offline. An integrated adaptive
dispatcher has not been benchmarked: HW popcount/dispatch, cache behavior, and
maintaining both decoder workspaces can change actual latency and memory use.
A CPU63 timing attempt was roughly twice as slow for all decoders including PM;
its data are retained but not used here. CPU62 matches the core used for the
previous Libra latency benchmark. Both variants use the same core and shots.

Implementation, verification, and reproducing these experiments
===============================================================
New source/API: src/decoder/libra_variant.h and src/decoder/libra_variant.cpp.
Defaults reproduce original PyMatching-backed Libra. Config switches:
  correlated_matching=true/false; large_gap_fallback=true/false.
Current stable original decoder remains src/decoder/libra_pymatching.cpp;
its large-gap fallback was retained. New variants are experimental alternatives.
New evaluator: main/libra_variant_evaluate.cpp. Build: libra_variants/CMakeLists.txt.
Usage and output-column descriptions: libra_variants/README.md.
Validation: tests/libra_variant_test.cpp passed on 6240 paired surface-code shots
including 96 forced-ensemble shots, with exact default-versus-preserved agreement
for predictions, assignments, class weights, gaps, and class masses. Both-class
and returned-assignment syndrome checks passed for all matching/fallback variants.
Fifteen independent-DEM syndromes also agreed between matching APIs. Output
histogram totals, failure traces, and cumulative threshold counters were checked.
Model dimensions are cached in the evaluator and test, avoiding repeated DEM
scans. The decoder changes only the two explicit options and initial-prediction
diagnostics; assignment and synthesis algorithms match the preserved source.
Build:
  cmake -S libra_variants -B .agentic_artifacts/libra_variants/build
    -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release -DENABLE_MPI=OFF
    -DCMAKE_CXX_COMPILER=/nethome/svittal8/gcc/bin/g++
  cmake --build .agentic_artifacts/libra_variants/build
    --target libra_variant_evaluate libra_variant_test -j 16
  ctest --test-dir .agentic_artifacts/libra_variants/build -R '^libra_variant_' --output-on-failure
Evaluator CLI (PREFIX must not already exist):
  libra_variant_evaluate fallback|correlation DIST WORKERS MAX_SHOTS SAMPLE_SEED
    PREFIX ERROR_TARGET KEEP_FALLBACK [ENSEMBLE_SEED]
Accuracy arguments: 64 10000000 20261001 PREFIX 20 1; holdout sample seed20261002
with explicit final ensemble seed20261001. Pin accuracy jobs to CPUs0-63.
Timing: correlation DIST 1 8192 20261003 PREFIX 999999 1 20261001, pinned CPU62.
The sample seed and ensemble seed default to the same value when the final
argument is omitted. Holdout explicitly retains ensemble priors.

  production/correlation_d{5,7,9}.*: correlation substitution / selection dataset.

The prior full d5..17 comparison, rare-event sampling, and GPU resource discussion
remain in Libra.txt and Libra-PyMatching.txt. This follow-up makes no new claims
about high-distance error rates, rare-event sampling, or a GPU implementation.
