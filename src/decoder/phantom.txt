Phantom cluster identification: software and FPGA notes
=====================================================

Current software implementation
-------------------------------
For a syndrome with H fired detectors, make an undirected graph with one vertex
per fired detector. Vertices i and j are adjacent when their pairwise affinity
is at most the current threshold. The software implementation uses root-based
union-find to obtain connected components. It decodes components of size at
most ASTREA_MAX_HW (currently 10) and skips larger components. The affinity
threshold selection and per-cluster PyMatching calls are separate from the
component-finding method.

Why the original label update was insufficient
----------------------------------------------
Writing parent[max(i,j)] = min(i,j) can overwrite a previous connection. For
H=3 and affinities A(0,2)=A(1,2)=1, A(0,1)=2, threshold 1, the pair (1,2)
sets parent[2]=1 and the later pair (2,0) overwrites it with parent[2]=0.
The resulting labels split one connected component. The original cyclic pair
loop also visits no pairs when H=2 and misses opposite pairs for even H.
Union of the current roots addresses both problems when all unordered pairs
are visited.

Hardware ideas discussed; no FPGA implementation selected
---------------------------------------------------------
H denotes the syndrome's fired-detector count, expected to reach roughly
150-200. ASTREA_MAX_HW=10 is the maximum component size to correct, not the
maximum syndrome weight. A hardware bitmask for detector membership therefore
needs capacity for the supported maximum H.

One approach grows a membership bitmask from every fired detector. In a
synchronous round, a candidate ORs in neighbors of all detectors currently in
its mask, using only masks from the previous round. For a connected component
of size at most 10, the candidate converges within 9 expansion rounds. For a
larger component, every candidate sees at least 11 vertices within 10 rounds
and can be rejected. Identical completed masks are duplicates; only their
lowest-index member need emit a cluster. At H=200, one bank of 200 masks of
200 bits is about 5 KB. The OR routing and adjacency bandwidth, rather than
the mask storage, are the principal concern if every candidate grows at once.
A fixed number of concurrent candidates could trade latency for less hardware.

Another approach stores a membership mask for each current cluster and ORs
two masks on a merge. A CAM can find rows with either old mask and broadcast
the merged mask to all members. This works if every member of a component
always has the same mask and updates are coordinated. A narrower variant
stores a cluster ID per detector (8 bits suffice for 200 initial IDs), then
uses a CAM to rewrite all members of one old ID to the merged ID. Both avoid
parent-pointer chasing, but simultaneous merges that involve the same current
cluster require arbitration or a combined update.

Odd/even scheduling by detector index can make detector pairs disjoint, with
special handling for an odd H. It does not guarantee that current clusters
are disjoint: after (0,1), (2,3), ... have merged, the next-stage edges
(1,2), (3,4), ... may each touch the same current cluster through different
member detectors. Thus a fixed two- or three-stage edge schedule alone does
not settle overlapping CAM writes. The schedule would need to account for
current cluster IDs, or updates would need to propagate until they converge.
