The decoder implements the matching synthesis algorithm in Sections III-V of
C. Jones, "Improved accuracy for decoding surface codes with matching
synthesis", docs/papers/libra-2408.12135.pdf (arXiv:2408.12135):

* Recover physical hyperedge detector/observable parity from the decomposed
  Stim DEM. Split channels by CSS graph component, rather than trusting the
  separator pieces: some Stim decompositions contain redundant graph pieces.
  Merge channels with identical full parity using Bernoulli XOR probabilities.
* Initialize both logical classes with correlated complementary matching on
  the original prior. Folding L0 into an explicit detector constrains parity.
* Convert matched graph edges to hyperedges using a minimum cost assignment
  with single channels and correlated pairs. Independent connected assignment
  components are solved separately with Blossom V and private boundary dummies.
  Assignment costs are quantized at 1e-6; evaluations use original double
  hyperedge weights ln((1-p_i)/p_i).
* Invoke a fixed ensemble of 60 correlated, two-pass PyMatching decoders when
  the original assignment weight gap is below 20 dB. Half the ensemble uses
  probability perturbations exp(Normal(0,ln(2)^2)); half uses ln(4).
  Perturbed probabilities are clipped below 1/2 to keep matcher weights
  nonnegative. Perturbed priors generate candidates; original priors score them.
* Split symmetric differences into components connected through detectors.
  Apply improving observable-neutral components. For the one-observable case,
  pairs of logical components also make valid neutral cycles; the minimum cost
  even subset gives the improving choice.
* Retain up to 30 distinct small positive cycles per logical class, clearing
  the heap when its base changes. Perform two synthesis passes. Connect stored
  cycles by shared hyperedges; enumerate each group's unique GF(2) span when it
  has fewer than 10 cycles, otherwise use singleton and pair combinations.
  Multiply independent group masses, using log arithmetic for stable comparison.

This is the paper's Libra with degeneracy estimation. It is a heuristic,
not an exact maximum likelihood decoder. The empty syndrome returns identity.
For a complementary gap of at least 20 dB, it returns the original
unconstrained correlated MWPM prediction, as in the paper's experiments.
The ensemble is fixed across shots; its seed is fixed across worker instances.
These implementation choices mean this is not a byte-for-byte
reproduction of the author's implementation or published experiment.

Scope: one observable, at most two graphlike CSS components per channel,
observable crossings on single-detector boundary components, and an independent
single-edge channel for each component. Unsupported models are rejected.
One mutable decoder instance is required per worker. No observed logical truth
is used to select corrections.

Validation
----------
All four Release CTests pass:
  libra_small_hypergraph_oracle
  libra_conditional_sampler_oracle
  libra_splitting_sampler_oracle
  libra_local_splitting_sampler_oracle

The decoder oracle exhaustively enumerates 512 configurations of a correlated
nine-channel model. Both logical classes attain the exact minimum weight for
all 15 nonempty syndromes. Degeneracy masses are bounded by the corresponding
exact class masses and do not count duplicate span elements. A truth-independence
test checks that changing the supplied observable cannot change the correction.
An independent correlated matcher checks the large-gap fallback prediction
for all 15 nonempty syndromes of the same small model.
Additional forced-ensemble tests check returned detector parity, observable
parity, and deterministic repeated decoding for 64 shots at every odd distance
from 3 through 17. These are consistency and small-model accuracy checks; Libra
does not promise globally optimal corrections for arbitrary large syndromes.

The conditional sampler is checked against an unequal-probability enumerable
model, its exact count polynomial, all possible counts, and 300,000 conditional
draws. The splitting kernel is checked on an enumerable model with 300,000
Metropolis steps for both the conditional distribution and likelihood ratio.
The local swap extension is checked against a four-channel exhaustive
conditional distribution over one million steps, including its bridge ratio.
Checks remain enabled in Release builds.

Experimental setup
------------------
All reported comparisons use sc_si1000(d,d,1e-3,false,true): rotated surface
code Z memory, d syndrome rounds, SI1000 physical parameter p=0.001, and
opposing basis detectors enabled. Stim decomposed detector error models are
sampled with their full correlated physical-channel parity retained.

The baseline is the repository's ordinary dec::PyMatching(dem,false), which
does not perform correlated reweighting. The paper compared to correlated MWPM;
the improvement ratios here refer to the requested existing PyMatching decoder.
They include the benefits of correlation handling and complementary matching
as well as synthesis and degeneracy; they do not isolate synthesis's benefit.
Every LER is per complete d-round memory experiment, not per syndrome round.
Both decoders process exactly the same sampled configurations, syndromes, and
observable truth. Sampling seed 20261001 is shared; worker streams are derived
from it, and Libra's ensemble seed is the same for every worker. Parallel
stopping and scheduling can change the final shot count between reruns.

502 GiB RAM. Evaluations use at most 64 worker cores. Compiler:
/nethome/svittal8/gcc/bin/g++ (GCC 15), Release, native architecture and LTO

Rare event sampler correction and method
---------------------------------------
Flagged for follow-up: src/sampler.cpp samples uniformly chosen k-element
subsets in generate_syndromes_with_k_errors. For unequal probabilities the
correct conditional probability is proportional to
  product_{i in subset} p_i/(1-p_i),
not uniform. SI1000 DEM channels have unequal probabilities. The count
polynomial alone does not correct this mismatch. src/sampler.tpp also starts
at the modal count and skips lower counts, and truncates the upper range;
the omitted failure contribution is not automatically zero. The original
files were not modified and their subset sampler was not used for these rates.
It additionally skips count levels whose probability is below the running
LER estimate, without an aggregate bound on their omitted contributions.

src/libra_rare_event.h supplies exact fixed-count conditional sampling via a
normalized Poisson binomial convolution tree. It reuses the correct count
polynomial routine from sampler.h. The rare_mix evaluator adds proposal
weights and reports its omitted count tail. Conditioning only on total count
is inefficient at large distances: many unrelated errors raise the total count
without forming a logical fault. For production results a second corrected
method, failure-conditioned likelihood-ratio splitting, is used.

Splitting intermediate priors multiply each ORIGINAL DEM channel's odds by
exp(theta); the decoder prior always stays at p=0.001. The initial theta=0.8
distribution is sampled independently with Stim until both decoders reach 25
failures. Subsequent distributions decrease theta to zero in eight steps.
The event sampled is the UNION of PyMatching and Libra failures, so both
decoders are compared on the same Markov states. A birth/death Metropolis
kernel has the exact conditional distribution as its invariant distribution.

For n active raw DEM instructions, the likelihood ratio is
  R = exp((theta_next-theta)*n + log M(theta) - log M(theta_next)),
  M(theta) = product_i (1-p_i+p_i*exp(theta)).
At each bridge, workers keep four independent ratio-weighted reservoirs and
resample the next population in proportion to total ratio weight. The primary
decoder estimates use the last bridge's mean(R * decoder_failure), multiplied
by the union rate before that bridge. Endpoint theta=0 labels provide a
separate mixing check. No count tail is omitted by this splitting method.

A reassessment adds local count-preserving swaps with probability 1/2,
leaving global births and deaths with probability 1/4 each. Pick an active
instruction a uniformly, then an inactive instruction b sharing a detector
with a, weighted by odds_b. Let Z_a be the total eligible neighbor odds before
the swap and Z_b the reverse total after it. The target odds ratio cancels
the proposal odds ratio, giving acceptance min(1,Z_a/Z_b). Moves leaving the
failure union are rejected. This gives the same target and bridge ratios,
with more opportunities to move nearby fault configurations. Final runs also
use systematic population resampling: one random CDF offset followed by evenly
spaced weighted draws over the four reservoirs per worker, then shuffled
assignment to workers. This reduces finite population drift. Sampling stream
seeds use a bijective SplitMix64 mapping of distinct phase/worker keys in a
domain separate from the ensemble seed.

Each production run uses 64 parallel chains, 512 recorded states per chain
per stage, 1,000 burn-in proposals per stage, and four proposals between records.
The initial independent error targets are 25 per decoder; the many conditional
failure records are correlated and are not additional independent rare errors.
Finite chain mixing, finite population resampling, and the error-based stopping
of the initial pool all introduce uncertainty. Worker-weight ESS measures
importance-weight balance, not the number of independent Markov samples.

Performance optimizations
-------------------------
Implemented cheap decoder optimizations include sparse bit scanning of the
syndrome, sparse symmetric differences, touched-detector scratch instead of
clearing the entire detector graph, direct singleton/two-vertex assignments,
and exclusion of correlated pair assignments that cost more than two singles.
The last exclusion is exact: replacing an expensive pair by the two private
single assignments always decreases cost. Consecutive duplicate candidates are
skipped; nonconsecutive duplicates remain because the synthesis base may change.
Match graphs and random perturbations are cached and prepared once per worker.
The paper's gap gate makes the expensive ensemble rare on ordinary low-noise
shots. Unique cycle spans and log sums avoid duplicate probability counting
and numerical underflow.

A preliminary same-seed d=7 ablation before profitable-pair pruning and duplicate
skipping measured 171.122 us, versus 168.118 us after them (about 1.8% lower average).
The synthesis phase decreased from 13.13 ms to 10.74 ms across 65,536 shots.
Both versions recorded 24 PyMatching and 9 Libra failures on that latency
sample. This modest average timing difference is subject to core/run variation;
the source-level work reduction is more useful on ensemble-triggering cases.
That ablation preceded the correction to the paper's large-gap fallback and
does not measure the final decoder. Final decoder latencies are below.

The fast splitting evaluator first tests PyMatching during a Metropolis
proposal. A PyMatching failure already proves union membership, so that
proposal can skip Libra. EVERY recorded state is decoded with both
decoders before accumulating weighted indicators. The transition
distribution and estimator are unchanged. This shortcut speeds the
evaluation rather than changing Libra. All final rare event runs use
this shortcut together with the local-swap mixture.

GPU parallelism and feasibility
------------------------------
Easy parallel work:
* Independent shots and the 60 ensemble matching jobs for a hard shot.
* The two constrained complementary matching jobs, with separate workspaces.
  The unconstrained correlated fallback can also run independently once its
  gate is known; running all three before the gate trades work for parallelism.
* Graph correlation rule updates, detector incidence processing, sparse
  differences, component labeling, and component/relative-weight reductions.
* Independent assignment components, and the two class synthesis chains.
* Independent overlap groups and the <=512 unique configurations of a group
  with fewer than 10 cycles. Large groups use at most 466 singleton/pair/null
  candidates before deduplication when the entire 30-cycle heap is connected.

Concrete loop sites are Impl::prepare (independent ensemble members),
Impl::candidate (correlation updates and edge-to-channel translation),
Impl::assign (independent assignment blocks), Impl::difference (detector
incidence and component weights), and Impl::log_mass (independent overlap
groups and relative-weight sums). Each class's synthesize calls depend on its
updated representative, so their order is preserved within a class.
Threading the current CPU ensemble loop requires private assignment scratch:
Impl::edge_position is shared today. Parallel class synthesis also requires
private detector-owner scratch. Overlapping correlation updates need per-edge
minimum reductions before starting the next matching pass.

Sequential work remains inside each sparse blossom matcher's priority queue,
alternating-tree updates, blossom contraction/expansion, and inside the ordered
synthesis updates for each class. Parallelizing weight reductions is easy;
efficiently parallelizing a single matching job is substantially harder.
The paper's binary-tree synthesis offers logarithmic depth, but changes the
heuristic synthesis order and requires another accuracy evaluation. It should
not be assumed to produce the identical CPU prediction.

The whole algorithm can be implemented on a GPU: it consists of graph matching,
component processing, XOR, reductions, and bounded cycle enumeration. A useful
port needs fixed-array graph/blossom workspaces, explicit queues, index-based
memory pools, and batched kernels. The current CPU PyMatching and Blossom V
objects use pointers, dynamic allocations, and host containers, and cannot be
used directly in a CUDA kernel. Tensor cores do not naturally accelerate these
irregular graph operations. Many independent shots are needed to hide memory
latency and divergence. GPU speedup and register counts cannot be determined
from FLOPS specifications alone.
A persistent queue of matching jobs can use available SMs for members of
ensemble-heavy shots. This handles uneven work from gap gating and reduces
the stragglers seen with one CPU decoder per Markov chain. Synthesis starts
after the required member outputs for a shot are complete.

B200 resource estimate -- explicit proposed CUDA layout, not a measured port:

NVIDIA lists 148 SMs for B200. Its SM 10.0 limits include 64 warps, 65,536
32-bit registers, 32 blocks, and 228 KiB shared memory per SM; B200 HBM is
up to 180 GB. Sources:
[NVIDIA B200 SM count](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/scaling-autonomous-ai-agents-and-workloads-with-nvidia-dgx-spark/)
[NVIDIA Blackwell tuning guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/archive/13.2.1/blackwell-tuning-guide/index.html)

Let D=detectors, H=canonical hyperedges, E=graph edges, R=directed correlation
rule entries, and B=ceil(H/8). The graph/prior tables are shared by all shots.
The model budgets 32-bit graph indices and quantized matching weights, double
original/perturbed hyperedge weights, and bitsets for candidate/cycle parity.
Matching distance and dual accumulators use 64 bits to avoid path-sum overflow:

  Shared tables T = 48H + 32E + 12R + 4(D+1)
                    + 60*(8H + 4E + 4R) bytes.
  Mutable per-matching-job workspace W = 256D + 32E + 8R bytes.
  Per-shot outputs/synthesis scratch S = 124B + 8H + 8D + 32768 bytes.
  Fully parallel ensemble instance = 60W + S.
  Streaming ensemble instance = W + S (matching jobs reuse the workspace).

The 124 bitsets cover 60 candidates, two representatives, two 30-cycle heaps,
and two temporary buffers. W is a proposed compact graph/queue/blossom scratch
budget reused between matching and assignment, rather than sizeof the CPU
objects. A port should use sparse maximum-benefit matching with unmatched
vertices permitted for hyperedge assignment, avoiding the CPU implementation's
materialized dummy clique. Dense dummy-clique storage can grow quadratically
on a giant assignment component and is not covered by this linear budget.
For these two-basis CSS models the assignment graph is bipartite: a correlated
pair joins an X graph edge to a Z graph edge. Its benefit is the cost of the
two singles minus the correlated-channel cost. A sparse bipartite matching
or min-cost flow implementation can replace the general assignment blossom.
Worst-case buffer occupancy and precision must be validated during the port.
These formulas are capacity planning assumptions, not a proven allocation bound.
The proposed original and folded baseline views share channel/rule tables and
remap logical-boundary endpoints instead of duplicating complete graph tables.
Cycle spans can use a GF(2) basis and <=30-bit coefficient coordinates for
exact deduplication; configurations are streamed rather than storing hundreds
of full hyperedge bitsets. These budgets cover the decoder, not Stim sampling
or the separate rare event Markov model and its local-neighbor tables.

How many instances can execute concurrently?
Under a concrete launch assumption of one matching job per 128-thread block,
64 registers/thread, and 8 KiB shared/block, registers limit residency to
8 blocks/SM: 65,536/(128*64)=8. Shared-memory and warp limits permit more.
This assumes a shared-memory carveout of at least 100 KiB/SM, sufficient for
eight 8 KiB allocations plus CUDA's per-block shared-memory reservation.
Across 148 SMs this permits 1,184 resident matching jobs, hence floor(1184/60)
= 19 complete hard-shot Libra ensembles executing all 60 members at once.
This compute limit is tighter than the memory budget at every evaluated
distance. The same launch can execute up to 1,184 streaming instances with
one active matching job each, while more workspaces wait in HBM. On ordinary
shots the ensemble is gated off; scheduling two complementary jobs plus the
original fallback simultaneously would permit floor(1184/3)=394 such shots.
Sequential reuse of a matching workspace supports the streaming count above.

These are design estimates of resident work, not measured throughput or a
hardware-fixed number of Libra decoders. For example 32 registers/thread with
a shared-memory carveout of at least 164 KiB/SM would allow 16 blocks/SM and
at most 39 complete 60-member ensembles, while
additional registers, larger shared allocations, or several blocks per matcher
would reduce concurrency. Kernels for synthesis and assignment consume their
own resources and can be scheduled after matching. CUDA occupancy measurements
and worst-case workspace tests are required to turn the estimates into actual
deployment limits. There is no implemented CUDA Libra decoder in this change,
so no measured B200 latency or concurrent-instance count is claimed.
